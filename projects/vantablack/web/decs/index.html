Detecting Coordinated Subnetworks in Multi-Agent Systems

Generative agents are increasingly deployed in shared environments. As demonstrated in the Hugging Face incident, a small group of coordinated agents can exert outsized influence on the system. Similar behavior has been observed on Moltbook: agents deployed with shared objectives flooded the site and coordinated through in-group rhetoric and interaction. We call a subset of agents whose behavior is repeatedly correlated a coordinated subnetwork.

This page demonstrates the detection and estimation of such subnetworks in a group of 2,574 Moltbook agents from January 30 to February 9, 2026.

Key takeaways:

  • the perspective space provides interpretable representations of the agents through time;
  • using all data through February 9, the method detects eight coordinated subnetworks of 4 to 10 agents, of which the agents in five subnetworks share a handle stem.

      Co-movement

      We first measure the co-movement of each agent pair at each consecutive time point. The statistic we use to determine coordination is a summary of the co-movement across time. Below are the summary pairwise co-movement matrix and the co-movement matrices for consecutive time steps. The block diagonals of the detected subnetworks are highlighted.       Detection

      We then search for the subset of agents whose summary co-movement is largest. To decide whether it is a coordinated subnetwork, we compare this test statistic to its values in 199 copies of the data in which agent labels are shuffled within every time step. When the test statistic exceeds the shuffled copies (p ≤ 0.05), we report a subnetwork, set its members aside, and search again. Below are the test statistic and null distribution of every round.

      null: 199 label-shuffled copiesobserved statistic (colored: subnetwork detected)
      Estimation

      For each detected subnetwork, we score every agent tested in its round against the detected set and fit a two-groups model to the scores: one group for the agents outside the subnetwork and one for its members. We repeat this until the set stops changing. Below, for each subnetwork, are the two-groups fit, the members' perspectives over time, and their co-movement at each time step.

      Data: the public Moltbook archive holds 3,482,649 posts and comments by 50,842 agents from January 30 to February 9, 2026. We only included agents with enough activity across the measured time period, leaving 2,574 agents and 1,768,042 posts and comments.
